# Machine-Readable Tables

## `fitsummary_machine.txt`

Full continuum-fitting table used for the paper's archival/SMA continuum compilation. The columns are fixed-width and correspond to:

1. Source name
2. Telescope/instrument
3. Observing frequency in GHz
4. Fitted-component or literature flux density in mJy; an asterisk indicates a 3-sigma upper limit
5. Observation date label in `yyyy/mm/dd_x` format
6. Synthesized beam FWHM in arcsec
7. Fit SNR
8. Archive filename or literature reference

For ALMA rows, `member.uid___...` filenames identify Member OUS data products that can be resolved through the ALMA Science Archive. For VLA rows, filenames are NVAS image products. SMA rows refer to the newly reduced SMA products generated by the scripts in this release.

This table is generated by `analysis/multi_freq_from_archive/build_fitsummary_machine.py`, which reads `fitsumfiles/sorted/fitsummary_fixtochosen_sorted.txt`, applies `continuum_product_alias_policy.csv`, `continuum_product_exclusion_policy.csv`, and `continuum_bandwidth_smearing_exclusion_policy.csv`, inserts the literature comparison points, verifies `continuum_source_roles.csv`, and writes `fitsumfiles/final/fitsummary_final_withextras.txt`.  The later 2009 NVAS reduction `BB0091` is the sole retained IC 2560 measurement from the 1998-01-06 BB091 observation; the older 2007 reduction is excluded from measurement calculations but preserved with both hashes and matching-header evidence in `continuum_product_aliases.csv`. Three failed NGC 4258 NVAS products are excluded from nuclear photometry. Two are replaced by the same-observation reprocessed detections from Doi et al. (2013); the artifact-dominated 2004-05-22 AH0847 image has no replacement row. `continuum_product_exclusions.csv` preserves all three hashes, headers, former rows, fit offsets, image-quality evidence, and replacement dispositions.

For final release, the builder consumes the frozen, exactly adjudicated 495-row
raw table, removes nine bandwidth-smeared VLA measurements and the one
retained-table IC 2560 alias, and adds 16 literature measurements. The result
is 501 rows: 485 local products (222 ALMA, 248 NVAS, and 15 SMA) plus 16
literature-only rows.  This result was regenerated from the authoritative Task
28 run and exactly checked.
The combiner also applies the bandwidth-smearing policy, so a fresh upstream
combination contains 486 rows; the builder accepts that prefiltered state and
produces the same 501-row release output.

`../validated_continuum_corrections.csv` records the cumulative 84-row
formatter/frame/earlier-noise and source-18 correction lineage. Its run-specific
preapplication snapshot contains 66 historical rows, including 50 flux-field
changes and 54 S/N-field changes with overlaps; Task 28 adds the 18 newly
admitted NGC 4945 products. These
come from ordinary zero-preserving decimal formatting, coherent J2000 anchor
coordinates for VLA/SMA rows, and use of the source-excluded image RMS once
rather than adding a related CASA formal fit error in quadrature.  They also
include the 27-row NGC 4945 fixed-nucleus rerun in
`../rerun_ngc4945_nuclear.py`: source 18 from Emig et al. (2020) is the
AGN/maser nucleus, while the formerly selected source 17 is preserved only in
`../ngc4945_component_audit.csv` as a rejected SSC/SNR-associated component.
The NGC 2960 85.88 GHz record is separately documented in
`../ngc2960_frame_correction_audit.json`: its historical `0.47*` mJy limit uses
the image-native ICRS fitting square.  That RMS-era classification is
superseded by the final scaled-MAD ledger.  The complete current change scope is
owned by the run-specific `reviewed_current_to_generated_deltas.csv` and
`reviewed_new_product_additions.csv`, not by the cumulative correction-lineage manifest.

The table contains the 21-source analysis sample and additional NGC 5495 catalog measurements.  Sample-level calculations use the 21 analysis sources.  A date suffix `_N` distinguishes additional image products for one source, frequency, and calendar date; it does not by itself imply an independent observing epoch.

The manuscript table name `tab2.txt`, the AAS production name `datafile2.txt`, and this repository file name `fitsummary_machine.txt` refer to the same continuum table in different contexts.

## `fitsummary_aas_mrt.txt`

AAS/CDS machine-readable version of `fitsummary_machine.txt`. This version puts the flux and FWHM `*` entries into separate indicator columns, replaces the literature VLA beam-size range with its midpoint plus a `*` entry, uses `SMA_this_work` for newly reduced SMA products, and preserves the fit-results numeric precision.

`AAS72796R2_datafile2_corrected.txt` is the corrected AAS production copy, and `fitsummary_aas_mrt_changes.txt` lists the corrections.

## Continuum product and source-role sidecars

`continuum_product_aliases.csv` is the generated audit sidecar for archive
products identified as reductions of the same observation.  It preserves the
retained and excluded product names, SHA-256 hashes, matching FITS-header
evidence, fit-table values, and the explicit selection reason.

`continuum_product_exclusions.csv` is the generated failed-product sidecar for
the three NGC 4258 NVAS reductions that cannot support nuclear photometry. Its
authoritative input is `../continuum_product_exclusion_policy.csv`.  It records
the immutable FITS identities and hashes, header values, off-nuclear free-fit
centroids, nuclear/image-quality evidence, former upper-limit rows, and the
two exact Doi et al. (2013) replacements. The AH0847 artifact record preserves
its former detection row and an explicit no-replacement disposition. The
reprocessed literature measurements retain their own citation and are never
represented as measurements from the failed NVAS products.

`vla_bandwidth_smearing_exclusions.csv` is generated from
`../continuum_bandwidth_smearing_exclusion_policy.csv`. It records the nine
off-axis VLA products for which
`x=(channel_width/center_frequency)*(source_offset/radial_beam_FWHM)` exceeds
the adopted maximum of 0.5, together with every numerical input and the raw-FQ
channel-width provenance. Four rows belong to NGC 3079, four to NGC 4258, and
one to UGC 6093. The remaining 248 VLA catalog measurements satisfy the rule.

`../continuum_source_roles.csv` is the machine-verifiable 22-source inventory:
exactly 21 sources have role `sample`, and NGC 5495 alone has role `ancillary`.

## `continuum_recovery_manifest.csv`

Portable one-to-one recovery inventory for all 501 canonical continuum rows,
generated by `../build_continuum_recovery_manifest.py`.  It records a stable
row identity, source/frequency/date, exact release-relative product or
literature citation, observatory-specific identifiers, and an authoritative
retrieval URL.  ALMA entries give Member OUS UIDs, NVAS entries give the
program token and exact filename, and SMA entries give program, observing
date, track, and CASA image path.

The 485 fitted products (222 ALMA, 248 NVAS, and 15 SMA) have audit-verified
byte sizes and SHA-256 values from `../continuum_product_checksums.csv` and total
6,040,874,990 local payload bytes; the 16 literature measurements have no
invented local-product checksum and are explicitly marked
`literature_only_no_local_product`.  `continuum_recovery_manifest_summary.json`
records the category totals and hashes of the final continuum table and
checksum lock.  CASA directories use `sha256-relpath-tree-v1`, documented in
`DATA_ACCESS.md`, so their digest is independent of the workstation root and
filesystem metadata.

The 89 added ALMA rows include the seven products from the initial inclusive
public-release census and the 82 products approved by the Task 28 audit of
filename-dependent selection.  The latter comprise 49 pre-cutoff omissions,
26 post-cutoff target continuum datasets, and seven Stokes-I MFS products.
The census, admission manifests, query/provenance records, and preserved
pre-fix materials are released under `../archive_inventory_audit/`; the FITS
bytes remain external.  Including these admissions, the authoritative external
four-pass inventory is 544 inputs: 222 ALMA, 307 VLA, and 15 SMA.

## `S230_extrapolation_comparison.csv`

Reference-flux table used for the revised 230 GHz analysis. It gives the selected 200--400 GHz reference measurement for each source, the adopted extrapolated `S_230_mJy` value using `alpha_fid = -0.7`, and the model range obtained from `alpha = -1.0` and `alpha = -0.4`.

The builder validates `../continuum_source_roles.csv` and selects reference
measurements only for role `sample`, so NGC 5495 remains a catalog-only source
even if a future continuum row falls inside the 200--400 GHz selection range.

For each source, the selection first uses the detection with the smallest beam
strictly between 200 and 400 GHz. Only when no detection exists in that range
does it use the smallest-beam upper limit; `flux_status` remains `upper_limit`,
and the extrapolated value must still be interpreted as an upper limit.

The table retains the selected telescope, observation date, beam, and a portable
repository-relative archive-product or literature reference (never an
author-local absolute path). `nu0_observation_id` is a portable,
deterministic identifier made from those physical observation fields; unlike
the displayed date label, it does not depend on the presentation-only `_N`
suffix used to distinguish same-day rows.

For detections, `sigma_S_nu0_mJy` is reconstructed as the table's rounded flux
divided by its rounded fit SNR, and `sigma_S_230_mJy` applies the same spectral
scaling.  For image-fit detections these are approximate local scaled-MAD
measurement terms inherited from the publication classifier, not Gaussian-fit
covariance uncertainties.  They do not include absolute flux-calibration,
spectral-index/model, source-model, aperture-choice, variability, or other
systematic terms. Upper-limit rows report these fields as `NA`.

At the historical pre-augmentation checkpoint, the corrected selected central
values were `0.780445`, `0.309386`, and `0.394812 mJy` for NGC 1320, NGC 3393,
and NGC 5765B, respectively.  At that checkpoint, the selected
flux/SNR-derived measurement terms were `0.018442`, `0.042617`, `2.030285`, and
`1.814934 mJy` for Circinus, NGC 1068, NGC 3079, and NGC 4258, respectively.
Correcting the
physical NGC 4945 component changed its selected
349.40 GHz input from `15.84` to `13.08 mJy` and its `S_230` from `21.2262` to
`17.5277 mJy`; its selected observation and detection status were unchanged at
that checkpoint.  The final augmented S230 table also selects the admitted
ESO 558-G009 X600 product: `0.66 mJy` at `313.11 GHz` in a
`0.71817 arcsec` beam, giving exact values
`S_230 = 0.8190724285773601 mJy` and
`sigma_S_230 = 0.09125138464542781 mJy`.  Its identity comparison uses
the Gao et al. VLA-B position as a nuclear proxy rather than an explicit disk
center; the J0648--1744 documentation lacks complete quantitative astrometric
QA, so the offset remains descriptive.

`../s230_anchor_identity_audit.csv` gives the exact selected observation ID and
physical-component disposition for all 19 S230 rows.  It links the canonical
NGC 4945 source-18 record and the rejected source-17 record directly to
`../ngc4945_component_audit.csv`.

## `s230_anchor_astrometry_audit.csv`

The 19-row component-coordinate and selected-image WCS audit generated by
`../build_s230_astrometry_audit.py`.  It is keyed to the exact status,
observation ID, and product reference in `S230_extrapolation_comparison.csv`.
The final audit contains 12 detections and seven upper limits.
For each detection it records the fitted/fixed component coordinate, the
authoritative nuclear coordinate or explicitly labeled proxy, a deterministic
ICRS transform, signed east/north and total offsets, separation in geometric
beams, positional-uncertainty availability, calibrator/QA availability, and an
evidence-backed identity disposition.  Upper limits explicitly have no
adopted component.

The actual selected-image WCS and product hashes come from
`../s230_wcs_evidence.csv`; source-level literature and identity decisions come
from `../s230_astrometry_policy.csv`.  The full schema, transform convention,
provenance, and limitations are documented in
`s230_anchor_astrometry_schema.md`.  In particular, separation in beams is not
a statistical significance when the component covariance or phase-reference
QA is unavailable.

## `variability_machine.txt` and `correlations_machine.txt`

Deterministic diagnostic outputs generated directly from
`fitsumfiles/final/fitsummary_final_withextras.txt` by
`build_variability_tables.py`.  These are not rendered paper tables.

`variability_machine.txt` contains the detection--detection pairs passing the
historical strict frequency and scalar-beam matched-pair screen.
`correlations_machine.txt` contains every within-source pair for the 21-source
analysis sample and preserves upper limits, beam ranges, same-day comparisons,
selection reasons, and likely duplicate flags.  Both products use
`../continuum_source_roles.csv` to restrict the calculations to that sample
while retaining all NGC 5495 measurements in the continuum table.
`variability_tables_schema.txt` defines every convention, including the
independent propagation of reported measurement-scale terms and censored
comparisons; backward-compatible field names containing `formal` do not turn
local scaled-MAD terms into Gaussian-fit covariance errors.

The final table contributes 498 sample measurements and 14,193 unordered
within-source pairs; NGC 4258 contributes 82 continuum rows.  The regenerated
strict screen contains 105 rows: 103 different-day and two same-day comparisons.

All three failed NGC 4258 NVAS rows are absent from both pair products. The two
Doi et al. (2013) replacements are detections, but the published
geometric-mean beams do not create new pairs under the strict 2.6 percent beam
matching threshold. Removing the nine bandwidth-smeared VLA measurements drops
two selected NGC 3079 comparisons and one selected NGC 4258 comparison; the
final count is independently generated from the 498-row sample.

The strict matched-pair screen is not a complete quantitative variability
analysis: same-day rows are explicitly non-temporal, no exact common-frequency
or uv/beam-shape matching is performed, and the propagated measurement-scale
terms omit flux-scale systematics and covariance.

`variability_likely_duplicates.txt` is a safety-net report for any probable
duplicates that survive the upstream policy.  It is header-only in this
release because the audited IC 2560 alias is already excluded and documented
in `continuum_product_aliases.csv`.  The legacy notebook and
`clean_variability_tables.py` are historical records and must not be used to
overwrite these deterministic products.

The ten-row manuscript excerpt of `fitsummary_machine.txt` is generated by
`../build_continuum_latex_excerpt.py`; it is not maintained by hand.  The
excerpt uses compact publication rounding, while this machine table retains
the full reported precision.

## `contamination/`

Deterministic 230 GHz thermal-dust and extended-jet contamination products
generated by `build_contamination_tables.py`.  The directory contains the full
candidate table, the selected-candidate table, continuum reference measurements, a JSON audit
record, the 20--2000 K modified-blackbody diagnostic, and generated LaTeX
tables. The final regenerated inventory contains 333 candidates and 14 selected
policy results.  The source-excluded scaled-MAD table classifies the NGC 1194
1.49 GHz input as a detection.  Its conservative broad-beam jet attribution
bound is `0.056341350943041435 +/- 0.007673842405753396 mJy`, or
`3.716543499121344 percent` of `S_230`; it is not a non-detection limit.

The generator applies the candidate-frequency rule relative to exactly
230 GHz, propagates each input's reported measurement-scale term with the same
factor, keeps upper limits censored, and retains telescope, date, reference,
and record-ID provenance.  Its literature inputs and adoption decisions are maintained in
`../contamination_literature_inputs.csv` and
`../contamination_adoption_policy.csv`.  For NGC 1068 the canonical table uses
Gallimore et al. (2024)'s summed 21.9 GHz HSA S1 model flux; the historical
central-pixel calculation remains a labeled, non-adopted diagnostic.
The CSV and JSON products retain machine precision.  Generated manuscript
LaTeX uses the common publication policy: two significant figures in numerical
uncertainty components, central values matched to the finest displayed
uncertainty place, and generally three significant figures for derived values
without an uncertainty or for model-bracket endpoints.
